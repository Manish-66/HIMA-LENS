# Cruden & Varnes (1996) & USGS Circular 1325 Taxonomy
### Reference Document for Technical Writing & Slope Engineering Dossiers

Sourced from Cruden & Varnes (1996), *Landslides: Investigation and Mitigation* (Transportation Research Board Special Report 247), and USGS Circular 1325 (*The Landslide Handbook—A Guide to Understanding Landslides*, Highland & Bobrowsky, 2008).

---

## 1. Two-Part Landslide Classification Matrix

Every landslide feature must be classified by its **Material Type** followed by its **Kinematic Movement Type**:

| Material Type | Fall | Topple | Slide (Rotational) | Slide (Translational) | Lateral Spread | Flow |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Rock** | Rock Fall | Rock Topple | Rock Slump | Rock Slide | Rock Spread | — |
| **Debris** ($>20\%$ coarse $\ge 2\text{mm}$) | Debris Fall | Debris Topple | Debris Slump | Debris Slide | Debris Spread | Debris Flow |
| **Earth** ($>80\%$ fines $<2\text{mm}$) | Earth Fall | Earth Topple | Earth Slump | Earth Slide | Earth Spread | Earth Flow |

*Complex Landslides:* Combinations of two or more movement types (e.g., *Rotational Debris Slump evolving into Channelized Debris Flow*).

## 2. Standard Morphological Anatomy

A technical report must identify and describe the constituent physical features of the slope failure using rigorous USGS / Varnes terminology:

1. **Crown:** The practically undisplaced material adjacent to the highest parts of the main scarp.
2. **Main Scarp (Crown Scarp):** A steep surface on the undisturbed ground at the upper edge of the landslide, caused by movement of the displaced material away from the stable slope.
3. **Flank:** The side boundary of the displaced material (Right Flank / Left Flank, viewed looking downslope).
4. **Minor Scarp:** A steep surface on the displaced material produced by differential movements within the sliding mass.
5. **Head:** The upper parts of the landslide along the contact between the displaced material and the main scarp.
6. **Body of Landslide:** The part of the displaced material that overlies the surface of rupture between the main scarp and the toe of the surface of rupture.
7. **Surface of Rupture:** The surface that forms the lower boundary of the displaced material (shear slip surface).
8. **Toe of Surface of Rupture:** The intersection (usually buried) between the lower part of the surface of rupture and the original ground surface.
9. **Toe (Accumulation Zone):** The curved margin of the displaced material most distant from the main scarp, encroaching onto the carriageway, river channel, or valley floor.
10. **Tension Cracks:** Open extensional fissures formed in the crown or body due to tensile strain prior to detachment.

## 3. Landslide Velocity Scale (Varnes & IUGS-WGL)

| Class | Description | Velocity Range | Typical Human & Physical Consequence |
| :---: | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **7** | Extremely Rapid | $> 5\text{ m/sec}$ ($> 18\text{ km/h}$) | Catastrophic; buildings destroyed; no time for evacuation. |
| **6** | Very Rapid | $3\text{ m/min} - 5\text{ m/sec}$ | Immediate danger to life; road vehicles engulfed. |
| **5** | Rapid | $1.8\text{ m/h} - 3\text{ m/min}$ | Evacuation possible; structures heavily damaged. |
| **4** | Moderate | $13\text{ m/month} - 1.8\text{ m/h}$ | Remedial structures can be designed; carriageway cracked. |
| **3** | Slow | $1.6\text{ m/year} - 13\text{ m/month}$ | Maintenance required; walls crack; road repaving needed. |
| **2** | Very Slow | $16\text{ mm/year} - 1.6\text{ m/year}$ | Imperceptible without continuous instrumentation. |
| **1** | Extremely Slow | $< 16\text{ mm/year}$ | Slow deep-seated creep; detected only by InSAR/GNSS. |

## 4. State of Activity Taxonomy

- **Active:** Currently moving (fresh tension cracks, displaced retaining walls, sheared vegetation).
- **Reactivated:** An old landslide that has resumed movement after a period of dormancy (typically after heavy monsoon rainfall or road-cut excavation).
- **Suspended:** Formerly active; has ceased moving in the current season, but conditions remain unstable.
- **Dormant:** Formerly active; no movement for several seasons, but failure causes remain unremediated.
- **Stabilized:** Artificially secured by engineered structural measures (soil nailing, plum concrete walls, deep sub-surface drainage).
