1. General
Regional Context • Himalayan Mountain Physiography

This Preliminary Project Report (PPR) establishes the statutory engineering baseline for the urgent stabilization and risk mitigation of the active slope failure along {{ assessment.telemetry.site_road_name }} (Chainage: {{ assessment.telemetry.location_chainage }}), situated within the {{ assessment.telemetry.district }} division of Himachal Pradesh at an elevation of approximately {{ assessment.telemetry.elevation }}. The road corridor serves as a lifeline transportation artery connecting remote mountain valleys, agricultural produce transit hubs, and inter-district trade routes across the Western Himalayan tectonic belt. Slope instability at this sector is governed by steep anthropogenic road cutting ($35^\circ-70^\circ$), intersecting joint discontinuities in weathered metasedimentary strata, and high antecedent monsoon saturation that periodically mobilizes colluvial debris across the formation width.

2. History, salient features and project overview
Failure Recurrence • Hydro-Meteorological Triggers

The project corridor traverses geomorphologically fragile slopes prone to recurring slope distress during the South-West Indian Monsoon (July–September). Historical records indicate that antecedent rainfall exceeding $65\text{ mm}$ within a 24-hour window induces transient positive pore-water pressures along the colluvium-bedrock boundary, causing localized tension crack propagation along the slope crest. Recent heavy rainfall events mobilized an estimated volume of {{ assessment.gabion_and_earthworks.excavation_cut_qty or '450 m³' }} of unconsolidated colluvial debris, encroaching upon the carriageway, blocking lateral lined side drains, and restricting traffic to single-lane emergency passage. Without permanent engineered intervention, progressive retrogressive failure threatens to undermine the uphill agricultural terraces and induce total corridor severance.

8. Mitigation measures
IS:14458 • FHWA GEC 7 • CEDD GEO Pub 1/2009 Standards
A. Structural Countermeasures
  • Plum Concrete Breast Wall: Construction of {{ assessment.retaining_wall_specs.wall_length_proposed or '30 m' }} long wall (Height $H = {{ assessment.retaining_wall_specs.wall_height or '4.5' }}\text{ m}$, Base width $B = {{ assessment.retaining_wall_specs.base_width or '2.25' }}\text{ m}$) founded on competent bedrock using $1:3:6$ nominal mix with $40\%$ stone plums ($\le 150\text{ mm}$) per IS:14458 Part 2.
  • Weep Hole Array: $100\text{ mm}$ dia perforated PVC weep pipes spaced at $1.5\text{ m}$ staggered c/c with $1:20$ outward downward slope, backed by $300\text{ mm}$ graded filter and non-woven geotextile.
  • Sub-Horizontal Pore Relief Drains: Perforated $\phi 75\text{ mm}$ HDPE drains installed at $5\%$ upward gradient (length $12-18\text{ m}$) into the rock mass per CEDD GEO Publication 1/2009.
  • Soil Nailing (Optional Upper Cut): High-yield Fe500D steel rebar ($\phi 25\text{ mm}$) in $100\text{ mm}$ boreholes with $30\text{ MPa}$ grout per FHWA GEC 7.
B. Non-Structural Countermeasures
  • Crest Catchwater Interceptor Drain: Lined trapezoidal masonry/concrete drain ($0.60\text{ m}\times 0.45\text{ m}$) constructed $3-5\text{ m}$ uphill of scarp to intercept surface runoff per IRC:SP:48.
  • Stepped Chute Cascade: Smooth hydraulic conveyance of intercepted surface discharge down the slope to cross-culverts, fitted with energy dissipator blocks.
  • Bio-Engineering & Hydroseeding: Hydroseeding with deep-rooting Vetiver grass (Chrysopogon zizanioides) combined with 100% biodegradable jute coir matting ($700\text{ g/m}^2$) per MoRTH Section 1400.
  • Instrumentation & Warning: Mechanical crack gauges across crown fissures; mandatory alerts tied to HIMA-LENS rainfall intensity thresholds.
CEDD GEOGUIDE 5 STATUTORY MAINTENANCE DIRECTIVE: Mandatory Pre-Monsoon Maintenance Inspection (PMI) by HPPWD Assistant Engineer before June 15 annually. All catchwater drains, weep holes, and chute cascades must be clear of debris blockage.
